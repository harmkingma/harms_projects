Ok now I want to add a flexible schedule for work. I want to add toggle buttons for each of the 5 workdays. E.g. mo,tu,we,th,fr. Because I go to the office only 3 to 4 days and the exact days which I go to the office change on a weekly basis, I want to be able to change the work day selection on the fly. Hence the toggle buttons. Also I want to add a "to work" departure time input. And I want to add an input for setting the amount of battery percentage consumed during a roundtrip to and from work. Finally I want to add an input to set the desired regular charge limit which is used when the car does not need to charge to 100% for battery conditioning purposes. And now the functionality behind the buttons: The rules: A round trip to and from work consumes about 26% of the battery charge in summer. In winter more. Probably 35%. Hence the input for setting the consumed energy. I want to have a minimum remaining SOC of 25 % after arriving back home from work. So, based on this information a calculation can be made how full the battery needs to be before departing to work. Example: Say my workdays are mo,tu and th. Then at the departure time on monday, the SOC of the car needs to be minimally 25 + (2 * 26) = 77 % in summer. Another example: Say my workdays are mo,wo,fr then the calculation becomes: 25 + 26 = 51% in summer because the next day I can charge again. Same example but in winter: 25+35  = 60% in winter. Another example: Say my workdays are mo,tu,we then the calculation becomes: 25 + (3 * 26) = 103% in summer. In winter it would be 25 + (3 * 35) = 130%. Now ofcourse, a SOC of larger than 100% is not possible so the target value needs to be clipped to a maximum value. And this maximum value needs to be read from the "regular charge limit" input. Now, all these examples talk about the required SOC before departing to work on the first working day of the week. In the examples always mo. But ofcourse this can also be a different day of the week. Now back to the "regular charge limit" input: this is the value that will be used to cap the required SOC calculation, ensuring that the car does not attempt to charge beyond this limit even if the calculated SOC is higher. However, there is one specific exception to this rule: At least once every 2 weeks the car needs to charge to 100% to maintain battery health. So this needs to be added to the automation also. The system needs to remember how long ago the car was charged to 100%. If this is 2 weeks or more, the next charging session should aim for 100% regardless of the regular charge limit. This "to 100%" charging session always needs to be done in max power mode. So when comfort mode is selected, charging to 100% is not allowed. Now the idea behind this automation is that I want to charge as much as possible during the days when I am at home to take advantage of the solar energy. However, it is very possible that at the end of the day before my next working day the SOC is not yet high enough (e.g. regular charge limit or the by weekly 100%). In that case it is necessary to charge the car to the required SOC during the night at maximum power. THe system should then calculate how much time is needed to charge the car to the required soc and calculate when to start charging to ensure the car reaches the required SOC just before work departure time. At max power the car SOC increases with 15% per hour (11kW charging power). THis way the car never sits for long at high states of charge. As the charging speed is not always fully the same a margin of about 1 hour needs to be built in. So the car should be ready one hour before work departure time. Another piece of logic: say that I go to work all 5 days, then the system symply needs to make sure the car SOC is as high as allowed on departure on monday (e.g. regular level or by weekly 100%) and check SOC during the week and recharge to the required SOC at max power during the night when it is lower than needed for one roundtrip to work. max power at night: Simply because there is no solar during the evenings and nights.
